# DeutschOS A1 EXAM COVERAGE & GAP MAP

**Source A (authoritative curriculum):** `a1-final-42-day-curriculum.md` — 6-week / 42-day A1 master curriculum, locked.
**Source B (authoritative exam evidence):** `DeutschOS_Session6_Final_Closure.md` and the underlying Session 1–6 exam-research chain (Goethe, telc, ÖSD, DTZ, DSD, TestDaF, DSH, ECL).

This map does not modify, improve, or extend the curriculum. It reports what the curriculum already contains, checked against verified exam evidence, and names only gaps that evidence actually supports.

## 0. A structural fact that shapes everything below

A direct text search of the full 5,896-line curriculum file found:

| Term | Occurrences |
|---|---|
| "Goethe" | 41 |
| "telc" | 0 |
| "ÖSD" / "OSD" | 0 |
| "DTZ" | 0 |
| "DSD" | 0 |

**The curriculum was authored with Goethe-Zertifikat A1 / Start Deutsch 1 as its sole explicit exam target.** Every "Goethe Connection" note, every exam-prep day in Week 6, and the Day 42 capstone are all built around Goethe's four-skill, four-Teil structure specifically. telc Deutsch A1 and ÖSD Zertifikat A1 — both named as **primary** exams in this task's brief — receive **zero explicit treatment** anywhere in the curriculum, even though the underlying German language content taught is largely exam-agnostic at A1.

This is the single fact that determines most of the gap register below: it is not that the curriculum teaches the wrong German, it is that it never tells the learner (or the platform) how Goethe's practice differs from telc's or ÖSD's, or that all three lead to the same CEFR A1 outcome by different routes.

## 1. EXECUTIVE VERDICT

**Is the current 42-day curriculum exam-ready in principle? — Yes, for Goethe-Zertifikat A1 specifically. Not yet for telc or ÖSD, though the underlying language competence very likely transfers.**

- **Well covered:** Goethe-Zertifikat A1 (Start Deutsch 1). All four Goethe skill components (Hören, Lesen, Schreiben, Sprechen) are explicitly named, individually drilled across dedicated Week 6 days (36–41), and assembled into a full mock exam on Day 42. Goethe's specific task mechanics — spelling names aloud (Sprechen Teil 1), question-and-answer cards (Sprechen Teil 2), visual-cue-card requests (Sprechen Teil 3), form-filling from a text (Schreiben Teil 1), a ~30-word personal email (Schreiben Teil 2), platform/time/price extraction (Hören), and short-notice/email scanning (Lesen) — all appear as named practice activities, not just as general language work.
- **Requires real changes, but narrowly:** telc Deutsch A1 and ÖSD Zertifikat A1 have **no exam-familiarity layer at all**. The curriculum's language content (numbers to 100, personal introductions, simple requests, form-filling, short messages) covers what all three A1 exams require at the CEFR level — this is a language-competence match, not a gap — but learners heading toward telc or ÖSD would arrive at test day never having seen telc's continuous (non-Teil-segmented) written format, its 15-minute paired-not-grouped oral exam, or ÖSD's separate Reading/Listening/Writing/Speaking timing (25/10/20/10 min) and its optional digital delivery format.
- **A smaller, second-order gap:** the curriculum never cross-checks its own vocabulary against Goethe's official A1 Wortliste (~650 active words). This is a verification/QA gap, not a content gap — nothing in the research suggests the curriculum's vocabulary is insufficient, only that no one has checked it against the official list item-by-item.

## 3. WEEK-BY-WEEK COVERAGE MAP

**Week 1 (Days 1–7) — Introduce.** Identity, greetings, spelling, W-questions. Directly seeds Goethe Sprechen Teil 1 (self-introduction, spelling) from Day 1, with a fully dedicated alphabet/spelling unit on Day 5 and phone-number dictation on Day 7. telc and ÖSD Speaking sections at A1 also require self-introduction content, so this week's language work transfers to those exams too — only the *exam-specific task mechanics* (telc's paired format, ÖSD's separate timing) are not addressed, because they're never named.

**Week 2 (Days 8–14) — Develop.** Transit, shopping, illness reporting. Seeds Goethe Hören (numeric/time extraction) and Schreiben (guided short messages). Content here (prices, platform announcements, short absence messages) is exactly the kind of material all three A1 exams test — Goethe explicitly, telc/ÖSD by content overlap only.

**Week 3 (Days 15–21) — Develop.** Conjugation, gender system, negation. Seeds Goethe Lesen and Sprechen Teil 2 (short-text data extraction, simple queries). No exam divergence risk here — this is core grammar, provider-agnostic.

**Week 4 (Days 22–28) — Develop/Recycle.** Accusative case, possessives, separable verbs, numbers to 100. Seeds Goethe Schreiben Teil 1 (Formulare) and numeric Hören. Numbers-to-100 coverage is exam-agnostic and satisfies Goethe, telc, and ÖSD equally.

**Week 5 (Days 29–35) — Recycle/Consolidate.** Dative baseline, spatial prepositions, pedestrian directions, clinic scheduling. Seeds Goethe Sprechen Teil 3 (polite requests via cue cards) and Hören (spoken coordinates). Content transfers to telc/ÖSD Speaking sections, which also test polite requests and everyday transactions, but again without any telc/ÖSD-specific task rehearsal.

**Week 6 (Days 36–42) — Consolidate/Exam-Specific.** This is where the curriculum stops being exam-agnostic and becomes **explicitly and exclusively Goethe-format**:
- Day 36: Goethe Speaking Teil 1
- Day 37: Goethe Listening Teil 1 & 2
- Day 38: Goethe Speaking Teil 3
- Day 39: Goethe Reading Teil 1 & 2
- Day 40: Goethe Writing Teil 1
- Day 41: Goethe Writing Teil 2
- Day 42: Full Goethe A1 Mock Exam (all four skills) + graduation capstone

No day in this week (or anywhere else) runs a telc-format continuous written mock or an ÖSD-format separately-timed mock. This is the concrete location of the primary gap.

## 4. A1 EXAM GAP REGISTER

Only gaps with direct evidentiary support are listed. All are **exam-familiarity gaps, not language-competence gaps** — the curriculum's German content is not shown by the evidence to be insufficient for telc or ÖSD.

### Gap 1 — No telc Deutsch A1 exam-familiarity content
- **Affected exam(s):** telc Deutsch A1 (Start Deutsch 1) / telc Deutsch A1 für Zuwanderer
- **Severity:** Moderate
- **Problem:** The curriculum never mentions telc, so a learner using DeutschOS as telc-track exam prep would reach test day never having practiced telc's continuous (non-segmented) 65-minute written format or its paired (not group-of-4) 15-minute oral exam. The language competence needed is already present; only the task-mechanics rehearsal is absent.
- **Recommended fix:** Add a telc-format familiarization pass, reusing Week 6's existing content, not new language material.
- **Earliest sensible placement:** Day 42 (or a new optional Day 42b/bonus session) — offer telc's official Übungstest 1 (`shop.telc.net`, confirmed free direct PDF in Session 2 research: `shop.telc.net/media/catalog/product/file/2/0/20210103_5070-b00-010106_web_1.pdf`) as an alternate full-mock option alongside the Goethe mock, since the underlying language is already mastered by Day 42.

### Gap 2 — No ÖSD Zertifikat A1 exam-familiarity content
- **Affected exam(s):** ÖSD Zertifikat A1
- **Severity:** Moderate
- **Problem:** Same pattern as Gap 1. ÖSD's distinct per-skill timing (25/10/20/10 minutes) and optional digital-exam format are never introduced.
- **Recommended fix:** Add an ÖSD-format familiarization pass reusing existing Week 6 content.
- **Earliest sensible placement:** Day 42, as a second alternate mock option, using ÖSD's official Modellsatz ZA1 ZIP (`osd.at/downloads/`, confirmed live in Session 1–2 research).

### Gap 3 — No cross-check against Goethe's official A1 Wortliste
- **Affected exam(s):** Goethe SD1 primarily (telc/ÖSD do not publish an equivalent word-count target, per Session research — this is a Goethe-specific practice, not a repository-wide standard)
- **Severity:** Minor
- **Problem:** The curriculum builds vocabulary thematically and does not reference or verify coverage against Goethe's official ~650-word Wortliste. This is a verification gap: nothing in the evidence suggests the curriculum's vocabulary is actually short of the list, only that no one has checked.
- **Recommended fix:** A one-time QA pass (not a curriculum rewrite) comparing the curriculum's cumulative vocabulary against the official Wortliste PDF, to confirm full coverage or identify a short list of missing high-frequency items.
- **Earliest sensible placement:** Not a curriculum-day placement — this is a backend QA task, ideally completed before Week 6 exam-prep days so any genuinely missing words could be folded into existing retrieval/recycling slots without adding new days.

### Gap 4 — Official exam timing/scoring thresholds not surfaced to the learner
- **Affected exam(s):** Goethe SD1 (and, if Gaps 1–2 are addressed, telc/ÖSD too)
- **Severity:** Minor
- **Problem:** Day 42 runs a full mock exam, but the curriculum text never states the actual official time limits per section or the 60%-aggregate pass threshold, so the mock may not train the learner's pacing against real constraints.
- **Recommended fix:** Add explicit timing/scoring context to the Day 42 mock instructions (exam familiarization only — no new language content).
- **Earliest sensible placement:** Day 42, as an instructional addition to the existing mock-exam framing.

**No Critical-severity gaps were found.** Nothing in the evidence suggests a learner following this curriculum would be under-prepared linguistically for A1-level German in any of the three primary exams — the gaps are entirely about exam-task familiarity for two of the three named providers.

## 6. EXAM PREPARATION LAYER

This is proposed as an **overlay on Week 6**, not a change to the locked language curriculum. It reuses the German the learner has already mastered by Day 35 and does not introduce new grammar, vocabulary, or communicative functions.

- **Keep Days 36–42 exactly as written.** They are well-evidenced, Goethe-specific, and should remain the default track, since Goethe is the exam the curriculum was actually designed around.
- **Add an optional "Exam Track Selector" at the start of Week 6** (a preparation-layer addition, not a language-content addition): before Day 36, let the learner (or the platform) indicate whether they are sitting Goethe, telc, or ÖSD. This determines which official mock material is used on Day 42 and which format-familiarization notes are surfaced (continuous-vs-segmented timing for telc; split timing/digital option for ÖSD).
- **Add short "format bridge" notes to Days 36–41**, one or two sentences each, noting where telc/ÖSD structure the same skill differently (e.g., on Day 37: "Goethe splits Hören into distinct Teile; telc runs Hören as part of one continuous written block; ÖSD times Hören separately at 10 minutes"). This is exam-task familiarity, not new German.
- **Day 42 becomes multi-track**: the same consolidated language ability is demonstrated against whichever official mock (Goethe Modellsatz, telc Übungstest, or ÖSD Modellsatz) matches the learner's actual exam target.
- **Do not** add new grammar, vocabulary, or communicative-function content for telc/ÖSD — the evidence shows none is needed.

## 7. FINAL RECOMMENDATION

**MINOR TARGETED FIXES REQUIRED.**

The curriculum should remain locked as a language-teaching document — nothing in the evidence supports a language-competence gap for any of the three primary A1 exams. The fixes needed are confined to the exam-preparation layer described in Section 6: adding telc and ÖSD as alternate Day 42 mock tracks with their own official materials, adding brief format-difference notes across Week 6, and running a one-time Wortliste QA cross-check. None of this requires reopening Weeks 1–5 or altering any locked grammar/vocabulary sequencing.

DTZ, DSD, TestDaF, DSH, and ECL are correctly out of scope for this A1 curriculum and require no action — their level floors (A2 or above) or access gates (school-restricted, integration-course-only) place them outside what an A1 learner following this curriculum would sit.
