# DeutschOS Pre-A1 → A1 Bridge — 15 short sessions

**Status:** Optional/recommended foundation for a near-zero learner. It is not an A1 curriculum, is not a replacement for the locked 42-day A1 curriculum, and does not change A1 Day 1 unlocking.

**Authority boundary:** The [final 42-day A1 curriculum](a1-final-42-day-curriculum.md) remains authoritative for A1 content, chronology, Daily German Core, grammar, and assessment. This bridge gives a learner a calm place to begin, then hands off to A1 Day 1.

## Purpose and format

- **Length:** 15 short sessions of about 10–20 minutes; skip any session that is already comfortable.
- **Outcome:** read simple German spelling cues more calmly, use a tiny number of familiar interaction formulas, hear and repeat short chunks, and understand that German learning will begin with small sentence patterns.
- **Not an outcome:** A1 grammar, a large vocabulary base, an exam result, a mastery claim, or a gate before A1.
- **Method:** listen/look → say → point/match → build a tiny chunk → stop. Use a notebook only for recurring letter/sound reminders and one useful phrase; do not copy explanations.

## What this bridge intentionally does not teach

- no article/gender system, plural system, case system, `ein/eine/einen`, accusative, dative, or preposition rules;
- no present-tense tables, `haben` teaching, broad `sein` conjugation, V2 explanation, negation rules, questions as grammar, subordinate clauses, or Perfekt;
- no family/home/routine/shopping/city/transport/work/appointment curriculum;
- no numbers, dates, time, price vocabulary, large word lists, diary work, exam preparation, or diagnostic score.

Those items belong to the locked A1 sequence when it introduces them.

## The 15 short sessions

### Session 1 — Start calmly

- Learn the study loop: hear first, look second, say a short chunk, then check again.
- Practice a neutral listening posture: do not translate every sound before repeating it.
- **Tiny interaction exposure:** notice `Hallo`, `Guten Tag`, and `Danke` as social signals only.
- **Hand-off:** active use and context begin in A1 Day 1.

### Session 2 — German spelling is read left to right

- Notice that German uses the Latin alphabet and that letter names can differ from English.
- Look at a name card; trace and read the individual letter shapes slowly.
- No spelling test and no required personal-name production yet.

### Session 3 — Five vowel letters

- Notice `a, e, i, o, u` in short familiar-looking word shapes and names.
- Practice listening for a clear vowel rather than swallowing it.
- Notice only that a vowel can feel short or held longer; do not learn a rule table.

### Session 4 — Umlauts and ß are spelling signals

- Recognise `ä, ö, ü, ß` as letters/signs worth noticing in German print.
- Gently round the lips for `ö` and `ü`; aim for intelligibility, not technical precision.
- Keep `ß` as a spelling-recognition sign; do not treat it as a new spoken sound.

### Session 5 — High-value spelling patterns

- Notice these patterns when they appear: `ch`, `sch`, initial `sp/st`, `ei`, `ie`, `eu/äu`, `au`, `er`, `qu`, `z`, `w`, `v`, `j`.
- Use the short learner-facing reference in DeutschOS; listen and repeat only the patterns in today’s examples.
- Do not memorise exceptions or phonetic terminology.

### Session 6 — A word can be built in layers

- Work through: letter/sound → syllable → word → short phrase → sentence-length chunk.
- Use a name or a familiar German greeting as the material; the point is the process, not vocabulary accumulation.
- Stop before grammar analysis.

### Session 7 — First social contact

- Recognise and repeat a very small set of approved social formulas: `Hallo`, `Guten Tag`, `Danke`.
- Practise choosing a greeting aloud with a calm, clear voice.
- **Boundary:** A1 Day 1 gives formal use, self-identity, and the fuller Daily German Core.

### Session 8 — Your name as a sound sequence

- Say your own name slowly, then in one connected breath.
- Point to the letters in your name; notice one hard-to-hear or hard-to-say letter.
- The full German alphabet and administrative spelling practice begin in A1 Day 5.

### Session 9 — Tiny sentence chunks

- Recognise that German messages are made from ordered chunks, not isolated word lists.
- Rehearse the already-exposed identity chunk `Ich bin [Name].` as a whole useful phrase.
- Do not explain verb position or conjugation here; A1 Day 1 does that in context.

### Session 10 — Yes, no, and a repair habit

- Recognise `Ja` and `Nein` in a slow spoken exchange.
- Practise pausing instead of guessing when input is unclear.
- Exposure to `Wie bitte?` / `Langsam, bitte.` may be used as a safety habit; their active curriculum use and chronology remain in A1.

### Session 11 — Listen for one thing

- Listen to a short, rights-cleared or learner-recorded micro exchange and identify one known item: greeting, name, or yes/no.
- Repeat one short chunk after a pause.
- No fake audio, no speed target, and no comprehension score.

### Session 12 — Say one thing clearly

- Choose one short familiar chunk and repeat it three times with clear vowels and a relaxed pace.
- Notice one sound or letter that needs another attempt.
- This is formative speaking preparation, not pronunciation scoring.

### Session 13 — Read a tiny exchange

- Read a two-line greeting/name exchange slowly from print.
- Point to the capital at the start and the punctuation at the end.
- Do not add new grammar or a reading vocabulary list.

### Session 14 — A practical starter rehearsal

- Rehearse a minimal opening: greeting → name chunk → thanks.
- Imagine a service desk only as a confidence cue; do not run the A1 registration task here.
- Write down one phrase you want ready for A1 Day 1.

### Session 15 — Enter A1 deliberately

- Review the learning habits: short aloud practice, look/listen carefully, build a tiny chunk, ask for repetition, and keep notes selective.
- Choose either **Start A1 Day 1** or repeat any bridge session that still feels unfamiliar.
- There is no bridge test, score, certificate, or completion requirement.

## Transition to A1

Start the locked 42-day A1 curriculum at **A1 Day 1**. The bridge does not unlock, delay, re-order, or substitute any A1 day. Learners who prefer to start immediately may skip this bridge entirely.

## Audit of the superseded 15-day outline

### Verdict

**Suitable after major corrections.** The old outline has a useful beginner intention and sound-first emphasis, but it is too redundant and curriculum-dense to be a Pre-A1 bridge. This separate document retains only the lightweight orientation role.

### KEEP FOR PRE-A1

- a sound/reading orientation: alphabet awareness, vowels, umlauts, useful spelling patterns, word-level reading, and basic stress/clear-vowel habits;
- listening and speaking from the first session, in tiny low-pressure chunks;
- basic greetings, name confidence, `Ja/Nein`, and communication-repair awareness as limited exposure;
- very small sentence-chunk construction and practical confidence;
- context-first, concise explanation, selective notebook use, and real-life relevance.

### MOVE / DEFER TO A1

- old Days 1–2: formal/informal introduction, `sein`, `haben`, statements, yes/no and W-questions → A1 Days 1–3;
- old Day 3: articles, gender, plural, and `ein/eine/einen` → A1 when scheduled; not a bridge topic;
- old Day 4: V2 and negation → A1’s staged structural work;
- old Day 5: number words, time, dates, schedule → A1 Days 10–12;
- old Days 6–8: routine, verbs, diary, family, people, home, location → later A1 content;
- old Day 9: accusative → later A1 only;
- old Days 10–13: shopping, city, transport, work/school, appointments, integrated service interaction → A1’s approved practical sequence;
- old Day 14 integration and Day 15 diagnostic → after relevant A1 evidence exists, never before it.

### REMOVE AS LOW-ROI FOR PRE-A1

- the first structured diary entry;
- broad verb-conjugation work and selected irregular verb scope;
- family/occupation/home/routine word inventories;
- full integrated A1 practice and a Strong/Developing/Weak/Critical diagnostic;
- grammar and vocabulary checklists that would make a near-zero learner wait before beginning A1.

### CORRECT

- The old document’s claim to be canonical is superseded and must remain so.
- Its original A0 → early-A1 label overstates an appropriate bridge boundary; this file uses Pre-A1 → A1.
- Full alphabet instruction is retained only as low-pressure familiarity; A1 Day 5 remains the approved active alphabet/spelling lesson.
- Repair language is framed as awareness/exposure, not an earlier active-core chronology.

### DUPLICATES A1

- The old Day 1 strongly overlaps A1 Days 1, 5, and 7.
- Old Days 2–4 duplicate the beginning of A1 grammar and communication sequencing.
- Old Days 5–13 duplicate the main practical/language content spread across the finalized A1 program.
- Old Days 14–15 duplicate the role of later synthesis/readiness evidence and should not precede the A1 sequence.
